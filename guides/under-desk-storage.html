Small homes · Workspace

Under-desk storage that doesn't steal your legroom

Storage under a desk is useful only if you can still sit comfortably, move your chair, and reach the things you need. Measure the working space first, then choose a storage style.

Updated October 2026 · Measure your own desk and chair before buying.

1. Measure the usable space—not just the desktop

Record the height from the floor to the underside of the desk, the width between legs, the depth before your knees reach the desk edge, and the area swept by your chair. Include brackets, crossbars, drawers, and cables that already occupy space.

  • Sit in your usual position and note where your knees and feet need room.
  • Leave clearance for chair wheels and any height-adjustable desk mechanism.
  • Check the full depth of a drawer when opened.
  • Measure the route for bringing a cart or bin into and out of the space.

2. Pick a storage style

Small rolling cart

Easy to move and useful when you want storage to double as a side unit. Check wheel locks, loaded stability, total height, and whether the cart can roll over your floor without snagging.

Clamp-on or mounted drawer

Keeps supplies close to hand without taking floor space. Confirm desk thickness, the clamp or screw requirements, clearance for knees, and whether the desktop material can support the attachment.

Open basket or bin

A flexible option for headphones, notebooks, and accessories. Open bins make items easy to grab, while lidded bins can look neater but may be harder to access while seated.

Under-desk cable tray

Best for routing cables and supporting compatible power accessories—not a substitute for general storage. Make sure it doesn't block ventilation or expose cables to chair wheels and moving desk parts.

3. A quick fit test

Before purchasing, mark the proposed unit's width and depth on paper or with removable tape. Sit at the desk, pull the chair in and out, and check whether your legs can move naturally. If a unit interferes with your posture, the extra storage probably isn't worth it.

4. Organize by how often you use it

Keep everyday items within easy reach, occasional supplies in lower or farther-away storage, and rarely used items elsewhere. Use small containers to stop loose objects collecting in one large bin. Avoid storing heavy items high up or creating a top-heavy rolling cart.

5. Keep the setup usable

  • Leave enough slack for cables and adjustable desks.
  • Keep vents, switches, and power connections accessible.
  • Check screws, clamps, wheels, and fasteners periodically.
  • Remove items that no longer belong at the desk.
